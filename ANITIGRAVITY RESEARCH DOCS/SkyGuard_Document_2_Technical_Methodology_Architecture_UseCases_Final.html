1. Executive Summary & Problem Formulation

This engineering specification details the system architecture and mathematical implementation of SkyGuard AI, grounded directly in RESEARCH_REPORT.md. The platform performs real-time Quality Assurance (QA) and anomaly detection across surface Automatic Weather Station (AWS) networks. Unattended field telemetry stations suffer from a spectrum of physical degradation modes:

  1. Electrical & Hardware Rail Shortages: Broken sensor wires or analog-to-digital converter (ADC) ground faults pulling readings to zero or rail limits.
  2. Telemetry Dropouts: GSM/LoRa transmission outages causing missing records (NaN values).
  3. Electrostatic & Inductive Spikes: Voltage transients, lightning strikes, or digital bit-flips causing extreme single-point excursions.
  4. Transducer Freezing / Sticking: Mechanical sensor lockup or ADC multiplexer latch-up where the output stops tracking natural atmospheric variance.
  5. Slow Calibration Drift: Electrochemical degradation or optical fouling causing progressive zero-point decalibration (+0.05 to +0.30°C/hr).
  6. Psychrometric Cross-Channel Breakdown: Radiation shield overheating or transducer cross-talk violating thermodynamic conservation laws.

Conventional anomaly detection methods fail in field deployments because they either trigger massive false alarms during legitimate dynamic weather events (thunderstorm cold-pool outflows, drylines, diurnal heating) or fail to detect low-amplitude calibration drift. SkyGuard AI resolves this fundamental trade-off through a 6-Tier Physics-Grounded Causal Bayesian Architecture that blends dynamic diurnal expectations, spatial peer consensus, sequential Wald-Page SPRT hypothesis testing, and thermodynamic physical invariants.

3. Dynamic-Adaptive Physics & Mathematical Formulation

SkyGuard AI is governed by dynamic data-adaptive computation and minimal physical constants. Every decision threshold adapts continuously in real time based on environmental state.

3.1 Dynamic Diurnal Expectation μt

μt = f(θsolar(t), DoY) + EWMAα(xclean) + Δpeer-offset (Equation 1)
Solar Zenith Angle Formulation: θsolar(t) = arcsin( sin φ sin δ + cos φ cos δ cos ω )
Where φ is station latitude, δ is solar declination, and ω is solar hour angle. Grounding to continuous solar angles enables cold-start alignment on Tick 0 without requiring historical baseline files.

3.2 Heteroskedastic Dynamic Uncertainty Budget σt2

σt2 = σsensor2 + σdiurnal2(t) + σspatial2(t) + κ · Δt (Equation 2)
Noise Floor Components: σsensor specifies transducer physical quantization (±0.10°C for Pt100 RTD, ±1.0 hPa for station pressure, ±1.0% for RH). σdiurnal(t) is solar radiation intensity modulated (peaks midday, narrows at night). σspatial(t) tracks live Median Absolute Deviation (MAD) among sibling cluster stations. κ·Δt represents causal variance diffusion across missing telemetry gaps.

3.3 Sequential Wald-Page SPRT for Low-SNR Calibration Drift

LLRt = max( 0, LLRt−1 + |μ1 − μ0|/σt · ( |εt| − |μ1 − μ0| / (2σt) ) ) (Equation 3)
Wald Decision Boundaries: Threshold A = ln( (1 − β) / α ) = 6.16 (with false alarm risk α = 0.002, missed risk β = 0.05). Standardized residuals εt = (xt − μt) / σt accumulate sequential evidence over time, catching low-amplitude sensor calibration decay (+0.05 to +0.30°C/hr) without false alarms during normal diurnal shifts.

3.4 3D Thermodynamic Psychrometric Covariance

DΣ2 = ztT Σ−1 zt ∼ χ32 (Equation 4)
Magnus-Tetens Dewpoint Boundary: γ(T, RH) = (17.67 T)/(243.5 + T) + ln(RH/100), Tdew = (243.5 γ)/(17.67 − γ)
Physical Invariant: Tdew ≤ Tdry-bulb + 1.5°C. Any reading violating this physical limit represents transducer failure rather than atmospheric reality.

5. Deterministic Tier 0–5 Priority Hierarchy

DecisionEngine.decide (model/detect.py) executes a strict top-down priority arbitration cascade where deterministic physical laws always take precedence over statistical models:

Figure 4: Tier 0–5 Priority Arbitration Decision Flow. Sequential evaluation from Tier 0 physical invariants down through specialist LLR, SPRT drift, 3D Mahalanobis, Isolation Forest, and Tier 5 spatial consensus veto.
  • TIER 0 (Hard Invariants & Rails): Evaluates electrical ground faults (0.00V) and absolute physical bounds (T ∉ [−40, +60°C], P ∉ [800, 1100 hPa], RH ∉ [0, 100%]). Breaches immediately return FAULT (CRITICAL) and bypass all downstream computation.
  • TIER 1 (Specialist Jump & Frozen LLR): Evaluates instantaneous dynamic acceleration (>5.0σ jump LLR) and F-ratio variance collapse over contiguous 4-hour windows.
  • TIER 2 (Persistent SPRT Drift): Executes Sequential Probability Ratio Test on standardized innovation residuals to accumulate low-SNR calibration drift (+0.05°C/hr) over time.
  • TIER 3 (Cross-Channel 3D Mahalanobis Covariance): Evaluates joint 3D covariance distance D2. If D2 > 16.27 (p < 10−3), flags multivariate thermodynamic breakdowns.
  • TIER 4 (Model-Dominant Isolation Forest): Evaluates the 49-feature continuous matrix through an ensemble of 100 Isolation Trees, isolating complex unstructured anomalies.
  • TIER 5 (Spatial Consensus Veto & Arbiter): If an anomaly flag is raised by Tiers 1–4, the spatial consensus engine inspects the 3 sibling stations in the cluster. If ≥2 peers exhibit matching directional rate-of-change movement, the anomaly is classified as a genuine regional weather event, and the alert is VETOED to NORMAL.

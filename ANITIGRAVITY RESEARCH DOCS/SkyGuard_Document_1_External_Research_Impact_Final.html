2. Automatic Weather Station (AWS) Observation Ecosystem

Automatic Weather Stations (AWS) have become the primary observation backbone of the World Meteorological Organization's Global Observing System (WMO-GOS). Modern AWS nodes measure Ambient Temperature (T), Atmospheric Station Pressure (P), and Relative Humidity (RH) at 1- to 15-minute cadences. In India, the India Meteorological Department (IMD) is executing an expansion of 200 high-density urban AWS nodes across Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, and Pune [R02], augmenting an existing national network of ~1,000 rural and synoptic observatories.

4. Research Foundations & Methodological Adaptation

SkyGuard AI synthesizes five foundational mathematical and statistical principles into a cohesive real-time detection pipeline:

4.1 Unsupervised Multi-Dimensional Isolation (Liu et al., 2008)

s(x, n) = 2− E(h(x)) / c(n) (Equation 1)
Where: E(h(x)) is the average path length across an ensemble of random partitioning trees (iTrees), and c(n) is the average path length of unsuccessful searches in a Binary Search Tree constructed over n samples.
Operational Adaptation: Deployed in Tier 4 on the 49-feature continuous matrix. Observations isolated near tree roots yield s → 1.0, identifying complex multivariate anomalies without labeled target data.

4.2 Sequential Probability Ratio & Cumulative Sum (Page, 1954; Wald, 1945)

St = max(0, St−1 + zt − k) (Equation 2)
Where: St is the cumulative drift statistic, zt is the standardized innovation residual, and k is the reference allowance parameter.
Operational Adaptation: Deployed in Tier 2 (Pre-Whitened SPRT Drift) on innovation residuals. Accumulates evidence over consecutive observations to flag slow calibration drift (+0.05°C/hr) before static physical bounds are breached.

4.3 Clausius-Clapeyron Psychrometric Boundary (Tetens Formulation)

es(T) = 6.112 · exp( 17.67 · T / (T + 243.5) ) (Equation 3)
Where: es(T) is the saturation vapor pressure in hPa at ambient temperature T in °C.
Operational Adaptation: Applied in Tier 0 and Tier 3 to compute Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD = es(T) · (1 − RH/100)). Flags unphysical observations where relative humidity and temperature diverge counter to physical atmospheric physics.

4.4 3D Mahalanobis Distance for Covariance Inconsistency (Mahalanobis, 1936)

D2 = (x − μ)T Σ−1 (x − μ) (Equation 4)
Where: x = [zT, zP, zRH]T is the innovation residual vector, and Σ is the empirical cross-channel covariance matrix.
Operational Adaptation: Deployed in Tier 3 to detect cross-channel breakdowns. If D2 exceeds χ23, 0.999 = 16.27 (p < 10−3), the system quarantines the multivariate paradox.

4.5 Additive Local Feature Attribution via SHAP (Lundberg & Lee, 2017)

f(x) = φ0 + ∑i=1..M φi (Equation 5)
Where: φ0 is the expected model base value, and φi is the Shapley additive feature attribution for feature i.
Operational Adaptation: Wrapped around the Isolation Forest via TreeExplainer. Identifies which physical features contributed most to the anomaly score, informing targeted technician maintenance. Note: Explains model decision space, not absolute physical causal proof.

4.6 Continuous-Time Differential Formulations & Sampling Cadence Adaptability

A critical architectural property of SkyGuard's mathematical physics engine is continuous-time sampling invariance. The system does not assume a static 1-hour sampling rate; all differential terms and uncertainty bounds scale dynamically with elapsed physical time Δt = tn − tn−1:

  • Continuous Autocorrelation Decay: ρ(Δt) = exp(−Δt / τdecorr). The autoregressive pre-whitening adjusts correlation memory continuously whether Δt = 1.0 h, 0.016 h (1 min), or 0.00027 h (1 sec).
  • Time-Gap Uncertainty Expansion (σgap): σgap2(Δt) = κ · Δt. At high frequencies (Δt → 0), temporal uncertainty smoothly vanishes, leaving only instrument quantization noise (σsensor).
  • Astronomical Solar Diurnal Grounding: Solar angles are derived continuously from UTC timestamps and geodetic coordinates, maintaining perfect diurnal alignment regardless of observation cadence.
  • Dynamic Derivative Jump LLR: Transient spikes evaluate instantaneous derivatives against Δt, testing rate-of-change (dT/dt) against thermodynamic physical acceleration envelopes.
